# Role & Collaboration

HeartNote is a collaborative product co-founded and built with **Ilay Admoni**.

This document exists to make my contribution explicit and avoid presenting a shared product as if every layer was my individual work.

## My primary ownership

My strongest ownership areas were on the product, brand, design and user-facing side:

- product concept and early direction
- brand identity and visual language
- UI/UX and information architecture
- interaction concepts for greeting templates
- gallery, customization, preview and sharing flows
- responsive behavior and Hebrew-first RTL experience
- content hierarchy, tone and microcopy
- front-end implementation and iteration
- product QA and visual consistency
- ongoing product ideas and experience improvements

## Shared product work

As a co-founded product, some decisions naturally crossed ownership boundaries.

Examples include:

- deciding which concepts were worth building
- balancing creative ideas with implementation constraints
- refining flows after seeing them work in the real application
- discussing product tiers and feature boundaries
- reviewing the live experience and prioritizing iterations

The value of the collaboration was not simply dividing a task list. It was continuously translating between product intent, visual behavior and technical reality.

## What I do not claim as sole ownership

The full HeartNote system includes substantial work beyond my primary contribution, including backend architecture, authentication, database design, AWS infrastructure, deployment automation and security engineering.

Those areas are part of the collaborative production product, but this portfolio case study does not present them as my individual work.

## Why this matters for my portfolio

Many of my other projects show end-to-end ownership. HeartNote adds a different and equally important product skill: **working inside a shared product and maintaining clarity around ownership**.

It demonstrates my ability to:

- communicate product and interaction intent to another builder
- make design decisions that survive implementation constraints
- give and receive feedback around real product behavior
- keep a coherent visual and UX system while the technical product grows
- distinguish personal contribution from team output

For Product Management and UI/UX roles, that collaborative layer is one of the most relevant parts of the project.
